# Panasonic vs Siemens vs NEC SL1000 PABX: A Tech's Honest Comparison

My background spans more than 15 years in telecoms and IT infrastructure, with Panasonic PABX, Siemens PABX, and NEC SL1000 listed in my vendor experience. That background informs the questions below; it does not establish a failure-rate ranking or prove that one brand is cheaper to support.

The short answer: compare the exact system, not just the badge. Panasonic and Siemens describe product families; NEC SL1000 names a specific system. For an existing installation, start with supportability and business requirements before deciding whether to retain, extend, or migrate it.

## Panasonic PABX

Record the exact model, firmware, cards, handsets, and installed licences. Do not assume that programming tools, SIP support, or voicemail options carry over between models.

- Can your maintainer export and restore the configuration?
- Which trunk and handset combinations are supported on this firmware?
- Can a local supplier provide compatible replacement parts and a written lead time?
- What additional hardware or licences would the planned expansion require?

## Siemens PABX

Identify the exact platform and its current support provider. A brand name alone does not establish multi-site capability, current software support, or replacement-part availability.

- Who owns the administrative credentials and configuration backup?
- Which software release is installed, and is support still available for it?
- If several sites depend on the system, what happens when the inter-site link fails?
- Will the support contract cover the exact cards, handsets, and integrations in use?

## NEC SL1000

Do not treat an SL1000 as an automatic recommendation for a new installation in 2026. Establish the installed configuration and obtain written confirmation of local support and replacement-part availability before investing further.

Several telecoms providers report NEC's exit from premises-based systems, with end-of-sale and support milestones.[^1][^2][^3] These are secondary reports, largely framed around other NEC platforms and markets. They do **not** establish an SL1000 support deadline in South Africa. Ask the local distributor to confirm model-specific dates, parts, and contractual coverage.

## What I'd actually tell you to buy

For an existing system, request two comparable quotes: keeping it supported for the next planning period, and migrating to a currently supported alternative. Include licences, compatible phones, installation, number porting, training, backup connectivity, and maintenance in both options.

For a new installation, require written lifecycle and support commitments. Do not buy old stock solely because the initial hardware price looks attractive. Check simultaneous-call capacity, extension requirements, remote-user access, and the cost of expansion against the exact proposed configuration.

A supportable system that meets the business requirements is a better starting point than an unsupported brand ranking. No affiliate program is approved for this comparison, and there are no tagged purchase links in this draft.
